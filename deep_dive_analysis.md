### 2. Why Did the Stock Fall Earlier?

JUBLFOOD experienced a significant de-rating from its peak valuation multiples (P/E > 100x down to ~60x–70x) and price corrections between 2022 and late 2024. The primary quantitative and structural triggers include:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        HISTORICAL DOWNWARD DYNAMICS (2022-2024)                    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. SSSG Deceleration & Flat Volume Growth (Post-COVID normalization)               |
| 2. Unprecedented Input Cost Inflation (Milk, Cheese, Wheat, Edible Oils)           |
| 3. Ind AS 116 Lease Capitalization Spikes (Depreciation & Interest Drag)          |
| 4. Popeyes & International Expansion Gestation Drag (EBITDA Burn)                 |
| 5. Aggressive Delivery Fee Competition & ONDC Threat Perception                   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

1. **Severe SSSG / LFL Deceleration**:
   Post-COVID surge demand normalized rapidly. Domino's India experienced flat-to-negative Like-for-Like (LFL) / Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG) for several quarters due to suburban consumer demand fatigue, lower ticket sizes, and reduced dining-out frequency.

2. **Gross Margin Compression via Dairy & Commodity Inflation**:
   In FY23 and early FY24, key input costs—specifically raw milk/cheese (accounting for ~35% of raw material basket), wheat flour, and palm oil—spiked by 18%–28% YoY. Due to high competition, JUBLFOOD could not pass on 100% of price hikes without damaging order volume, resulting in a ~300–400 bps hit to EBITDA margins.

3. **Gestation Drag from Secondary Brands & Popeyes Capex**:
   Heavy upfront capital expenditure and operating investments into launch infrastructure for **Popeyes India**, **Hong's Kitchen**, and **Dunkin'** created a temporary dilution in overall Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) and store-level operating margins before reaching scale economies.

4. **Acquisition Dynamics of DP Eurasia**:
   The strategic acquisition to gain full operational control of DP Eurasia (Domino’s Turkish, Azerbaijani, and Georgian licensee) involved debt loading and short-term earnings volatility, causing foreign institutional investors (FIIs) to temporarily de-risk position holdings.

5. **Ind AS 116 Lease Liability Expansion**:
   Aggressive physical store expansion (adding 200–250 stores annually) significantly increased non-cash lease depreciation and interest expenses, artificially depressing bottom-line Net Profit (PAT) margins under Ind AS 116 accounting norms.
