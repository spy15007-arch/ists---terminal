### 2. Why Did the Stock Fall Earlier?

Aarti Industries experienced a sharp derating between FY22 and early FY24 due to a confluence of macroeconomic, sector-specific, and operational headwinds:

1. **Global Agrochemical & Specialty Chemical Destocking:** Rapid inventory building during post-COVID supply chain bottlenecks resulted in massive oversupply across global channels. Key end-user sectors (Agrochem and Industrial) reduced purchases significantly in FY23–FY24.
2. **Chinese Dumping & Price Deflation:** Post-reopening, Chinese chemical players aggressively dumped excess capacity in global markets, leading to realization drops of 25%–40% in core benzene derivative intermediates.
3. **Elevated Raw Material & Energy Costs:** Unfavorable price spikes in Crude/Benzene derivatives during late FY22, coupled with high utility costs, compressed gross margins sharply before pass-through clauses could kick in.
4. **Capex Drag & Depressed Return Ratios:** Extensive capital expenditure (~₹3,000+ Cr over FY21–FY24) remained uncommissioned or operating at low utilization rates, dragging down Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) to high single digits (~8–9%).
5. **Loss/Cancellation of High-Margin Long-Term Contracts:** The termination of a major multi-year supply contract in late 2022 created short-term sentiment overhang and volume uncertainty.

### 2. Why Did the Stock Fall Earlier?

JUBLFOOD experienced a significant de-rating and stock price correction from its peak levels above ₹900 down to the sub-₹450 region over FY23–FY24. This multi-quarter drawdown was triggered by five distinct structural and cyclical headwinds:

1. **Unprecedented Input Cost Inflation (Dairy & Wheat):**
   Milk, cheese, and wheat flour—which constitute over 50% of raw material costs—witnessed 20-30% price spikes. Cheese prices hit decade-highs, squeezing Gross Margins from ~77% down to sub-75% levels, damaging operating profitability.

2. **Severe Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG) Compression:**
   Post-COVID demand normalization, coupled with urban mass-market demand fatigue, caused Domino's India SSSG to turn negative (-1.3% to -4.5% across multiple quarters in FY24). High inflation eroded discretionary dining budgets.

3. **Margin Dilution from Aggressive Network Expansion:**
   Despite top-line headwinds, management accelerated store additions (200+ Domino's stores per year). New stores required a 12-18 month incubation period, creating significant operating leverage drag on EBITDA margins.

4. **Integration Leverage from DP Eurasia Acquisition:**
   The strategic bid to acquire 100% control of DP Eurasia (Turkey, Georgia, Azerbaijan) required sub-debt funding and generated balance sheet expansion. Investors punished the short-term leverage spike and hyperinflation accounting uncertainties in Turkey.

5. **Disruption in Aggregator Delivery Dynamic & Hyper-Competition:**
   Emerging quick-service brands (Third-wave pizza operators, regional chicken QSRs) and high delivery fees on food aggregators (Zomato/Swiggy) fragmented urban QSR traffic away from Domino's proprietary app.
