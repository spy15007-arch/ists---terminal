### 2. Why Did the Stock Fall Earlier?

Redington experienced cyclical pullbacks and valuation compression during late CY22 and CY23 driven by macro-headwinds across its operating markets:

1. **Post-COVID Hardware Demand Normalisation & Destocking**: The surge in consumer PC, notebook, and mobility sales during 2020–2022 created a high base effect. Subsequent channel inventory destocking across India and META (Middle East, Turkey, Africa) led to revenue growth moderation and inventory markdowns.
2. **Gross Margin Compression in META Region**: Hyperinflation and currency devaluation in markets like Turkey (Turkish Lira volatility) and parts of Africa triggered foreign exchange translation losses and elevated financing costs to hedge exposure, depressing overall net margins.
3. **Escalating Global Interest Rates**: Redington’s business model requires working capital financing. The sharp rise in global and domestic interest rates pushed finance costs up from ~₹120 Cr to over ₹300+ Cr annually during peak rate cycles, directly impacting bottom-line profitability.
4. **Leadership Transition & Executive Realignment**: The departure of long-standing leadership created short-term operational uncertainty among institutional investors, leading to multiple expansion caps until the new management established strategic execution continuity.
